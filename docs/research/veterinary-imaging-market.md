### DICOM in Veterinary Imaging

DICOM is the standard in veterinary imaging. The same DICOM PS3 standard used in human medicine applies, with veterinary-specific extensions defined primarily in **DICOM Supplement 43** (2002) and subsequent work by **DICOM Working Group 25** (Veterinary Medicine).

**Key veterinary-specific DICOM tags:**
- `(0010,2201)` Patient Species Description
- `(0010,2292)` Patient Breed Description
- `(0010,2297)` Responsible Person (owner)
- `(0010,2299)` Responsible Organization
- Other Patient ID recommended for microchip number

**Conformance reality (poor):** A landmark 2018 German study surveyed 488 imaging studies from 115 veterinary institutions and found that **only 5.1% (25 studies) fully complied** with the veterinary DICOM standard tags. The veterinary-specific identification tags have found minimal acceptance among practicing veterinarians.

This means in practice, most veterinary facilities use human-oriented DICOM workflows and put animal/owner info in standard Patient Name/ID fields in ad-hoc formats (e.g., "OwnerLast^PetName").

Sources:
- Defined Supplement 43 and WG-25 goals: [PostDICOM - DICOM in Veterinary Medicine](https://www.postdicom.com/en/blog/dicom-in-veterinary-medicine), [dvm360 - Intro to DICOM](https://www.dvm360.com/view/introduction-dicom)
- Wright MA. "Introduction to DICOM for the practicing veterinarian." Veterinary Radiology & Ultrasound, 2008. [PubMed 18283981](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18283981/)

| Method | Prevalence | Pain Points |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| CD/DVD | Still common (~30-40% of referrals) | Slow, unreliable media, requires physical transport, often unreadable |
| Email/file transfer | Common for small studies | File size limits, no DICOM viewer on receiving end, lossy compression |
| Cloud PACS sharing | Growing but limited | Requires both parties on same system or compatible platforms |
| Teleradiology upload portals | Common for reads | One-way upload for interpretation; not bidirectional sharing |
| USB drives | Common | Same problems as CD plus security concerns |

**Major pain points:**
1. **No universal image exchange network** -- unlike human medicine (IHE, Carequality, CommonWell), veterinary has no interoperability framework
2. **Fragmented systems** -- each practice may use a different mini-PACS or no PACS at all
3. **Referring vet to specialist image transfer** is the single biggest workflow bottleneck in veterinary referral
4. **Long-term archiving** often neglected; practices lose images when servers fail
5. **Limited IT expertise** -- most veterinary practices have minimal IT staff
6. **PMS integration inconsistency** -- IDEXX Cornerstone, ezyVet, Shepherd, Rhapsody, Avimark all have varying imaging integration levels

## 3. Regulatory Landscape

### HIPAA Equivalent for Animals

**There is no HIPAA equivalent for veterinary medicine.** Animal patient data is not protected health information under US federal law. However:

- **Pet owner personal information** (name, address, phone, email) is subject to general consumer privacy laws (state-level laws, CCPA in California, GDPR in Europe)
- Without adequate anonymization and informed consent, clients may not know who their data is being shared with
- Veterinary medical records laws vary by state but generally require record retention (typically 3-7 years) without HIPAA-level privacy mandates
- PCI-DSS applies to payment data

Sources: [HNI - HIPAA for Pets](https://www.hni.com/blog/bid/88056/hipaa-for-pets-yes-medical-privacy-matters-for-animals), [Mahan Law - Vet Data Privacy](https://mahanlaw.com/practice-areas/veterinary-practice-consulting/data-privacy-and-security-in-veterinary-practices/), [Co.Vet - Records Laws](https://www.co.vet/post/veterinary-medical-records-laws)

### FDA/USDA Oversight of Veterinary Imaging Software

- **FDA does NOT require 510(k) clearance** for veterinary imaging software. While veterinary devices are technically subject to FDA/CVM oversight, pre-market approval is not required for veterinary devices. The FDA can take regulatory action if a device is misbranded or adulterated, but does not require the same pre-market review process as human medical devices.
- **Veterinary device manufacturers** must ensure devices meet applicable testing, safety, and labeling requirements, but the regulatory bar is significantly lower than for human medical devices.
- **USDA** does not regulate veterinary imaging software. APHIS oversees animal health broadly but has no jurisdiction over diagnostic imaging IT.
- In December 2024, FDA-CVM and USDA-CVB published a **Charter for the Jurisdiction Determination Process** to clarify regulatory boundaries, but this primarily concerns biologicals, not imaging software.

**Practical implication**: Veterinary imaging software can be deployed without 510(k), De Novo, CE marking, or any software-specific certification. This dramatically lowers barriers to entry compared to human healthcare IT.

Sources: [FDLI - Vet Product Regulation](https://www.fdli.org/2024/05/veterinary-product-regulation-an-introduction-to-the-roles-and-responsibilities-of-fda-and-usda/), [FDLI - Vet Drug and Device Requirements](https://www.fdli.org/2024/05/an-introduction-to-veterinary-drug-and-device-requirements-and-challenges/), [FDA-USDA Charter](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2024/12/fda-and-usda-announce-charter-to-clarify-jurisdiction-for-animal-biologicals)

### Veterinary Radiology Certification

- **ACVR (American College of Veterinary Radiology)** is the board certification body for veterinary radiologists (~400-500 diplomates in North America)
- State veterinary practice acts vary on teleradiology across state lines -- interpretation must generally be by or under the supervision of a licensed veterinarian
- No certification or compliance requirements exist for veterinary imaging *software* specifically

## 4. De-identification Considerations

### Is De-identification Needed?

- **For clinical use**: Generally no legal requirement. Owner name appears in DICOM tags but there is no mandate to remove it for clinical sharing.
- **For research/publication**: Yes -- research ethics boards (IACUCs) and journals may require removal of owner identifying information. Veterinary teaching hospitals with IRB/IACUC oversight typically de-identify images before research use.
- **For AI training**: Companies like SignalPET and Vetology anonymize client and patient data before training classifiers. Identifying details are removed while essential clinical context (species, breed, signalment) may be retained.
- **Burned-in text**: Less common than in human imaging, but some older CR/DR systems burn in clinic name, owner name, or patient info on images.

**Key gap**: There is a "marked gap between the measures taken to de-identify human and animal study data, with sparse regulation on how veterinary data needs to be redacted or formatted when shared between institutions."

Sources: [PMC - Vet Big Data](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8300140/), [PMC - Ethical AI in Vet Radiology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10107688/), [Vetology - Ethical AI](https://vetology.net/ethical-ai-in-veterinary-imaging/), [Springer - Vet Oncology Data](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s44356-025-00043-2)

### Research Data Sharing Barriers

1. **No standard data sharing infrastructure** -- no veterinary equivalent of TCIA
2. **Owner consent requirements unclear** -- sparse regulation on veterinary data redaction
3. **Lack of data standards** beyond basic DICOM (and poor DICOM conformance in practice)
4. **Resource limitations** at smaller institutions prevent managing large imaging datasets
5. **Incompatible data practices** between institutions make collaboration difficult
6. **No NIH DMS-equivalent mandate** for veterinary research (NIH DMS applies to NIH-funded veterinary research, but vet imaging research funding is small)
7. **No equivalent of BIDS** for veterinary neuroimaging

Source: [PMC - Vet Informatics AVI/COHA](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7382640/)

### AI Research in Veterinary Imaging

**Key research areas:**
- **Radiograph interpretation**: Thoracic, musculoskeletal, dental (33% of DL vet studies use radiography)
- **Cytology**: Cell classification for oncology diagnosis (33% of DL vet studies)
- **MRI**: Brain tumor detection, neurological conditions (8% of DL vet studies)
- **Ultrasound**: Cardiac, abdominal screening (5% of DL vet studies)

**Cross-species transfer learning** (a key research frontier):
- A deep learning fracture detection model trained on human data and fine-tuned with equine imaging achieved **96.7% accuracy** for modality classification, **97.2% for projection recognition**, and fracture localization IoU values of **0.71-0.84** across equine datasets
- A CNN trained on pig pneumonia images was applied to lamb lung pathology detection **without fine-tuning**, demonstrating zero-shot cross-species transfer
- Pre-trained ImageNet models transfer well to veterinary radiograph classification with relatively small fine-tuning datasets
- Models pre-trained on human medical images can be fine-tuned for veterinary applications, reducing computational cost and training data requirements

## Relevance to AEGIS

### Market Opportunity

If considering veterinary imaging as a market expansion for AEGIS:

**Advantages:**
- Far lighter regulatory burden (no HIPAA, no FDA 510(k) for software)
- Same DICOM standard (Supplement 43 additions are minor, and poorly adopted in practice anyway)
- Massive unmet need for cloud image sharing between referring vets and specialists
- Corporate consolidation (Mars, NVA/Ethos) creates large buyer groups seeking standardized platforms
- Growing AI opportunity with lower regulatory barriers than human medicine
- De-identification is simpler (owner info removal, no facial recognition concerns for animal subjects)
- Veterinary PACS market alone is $662M and growing at 7.1% CAGR

**Challenges:**
- Lower price tolerance than human healthcare
- Fragmented market with many small practices
- Less IT sophistication at the practice level
- Smaller total addressable market than human medical imaging
- Would need PMS integration (IDEXX Cornerstone, ezyVet, etc.)
- Established competitor ecosystem (IDEXX, Radimal, SignalPET)

**AEGIS adaptations needed:**
- Support for DICOM Supplement 43 veterinary tags (species, breed, responsible person)
- De-identification profile for owner PII rather than patient PHI (no defacing needed)
- PMS integration layer (or HL7/FHIR-Vet interop)
- Pricing model appropriate for veterinary economics ($200-800/mo cloud PACS range)
- Species-aware classification service (not just human anatomy)
- Teleradiology workflow support (the $250M+ sub-market is high-growth)
