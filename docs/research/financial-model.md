# AEGIS Financial Model — 3-Year Projection

Last updated: 2026-02-26

## Assumptions

### Market Assumptions
- Total addressable market (TAM): ~$600M (health data de-identification, 2026 est.)
- Serviceable addressable market (SAM): ~$120M (medical imaging de-identification + research data sharing, US + EU)
- Serviceable obtainable market (SOM): ~$2M–5M Year 1, growing to ~$10M–20M Year 3
- NIH DMS policy is the primary demand catalyst for research customers
- Enterprise radiology de-identification is the expansion market (Phase 5+)

### Product Assumptions
- Open-source core available throughout (builds adoption, not directly monetized)
- Hosted SaaS is the primary revenue driver
- Free tier converts at 10–15% to paid within 6 months
- Average contract length: 12 months (annual billing default)
- No on-premises license revenue in Year 1; begins Year 2

### Cost Assumptions
- Founding team: 2–3 people (Year 1), growing to 8–12 (Year 3)
- Cloud infrastructure costs scale at ~15–25% of SaaS revenue
- Cloud AI costs are passed through at 15–20% margin
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC): ~$2,000–5,000 (low-touch for research, high-touch for enterprise)
- Gross margin target: 70–80% on SaaS, 40–60% on services

## Funding Scenarios

### Scenario A: Bootstrapped

- No external funding
- Team stays 2–3 through Year 1
- Revenue covers costs by mid-Year 2
- Slower growth but full ownership retained
- **Risk:** Can't hire sales early enough, miss enterprise window
- **Requires:** $300K–500K initial capital (savings or angel)

### Scenario B: Seed Round ($1M–2M)

- Raise after first 5–10 paying customers (proof of product-market fit)
- Hire 2–3 engineers + 1 sales immediately
- Accelerate enterprise pipeline
- **Use of funds:** Engineering (50%), sales (25%), cloud infra (15%), legal/compliance (10%)
- **Expected dilution:** 15–25%
- **Investors:** Health-tech focused angels, SBIR/STTR grants, early-stage VCs (Rock Health, Launchpad Digital Health)

### Scenario C: Series A ($5M–10M, Year 2–3)

- Raise after $500K+ ARR and 3+ enterprise customers
- Scale sales team (3–5 reps), expand engineering (10+), international expansion
- SOC 2 Type II certification
- **Expected dilution:** 20–30%
- **Investors:** Health-tech VCs (a16z Bio, General Catalyst, Bessemer)

### Scenario D: Grant-Funded (Non-Dilutive)

- NIH SBIR/STTR Phase I ($275K, 6 months) → Phase II ($1M, 2 years)
- NSF SBIR ($275K Phase I, $1M Phase II)
- ARPA-H (Advanced Research Projects Agency for Health) — larger grants possible
- NIST cybersecurity/privacy grants
- **Advantage:** Non-dilutive, validates product with federal stamp of approval
- **Disadvantage:** Slow timeline (6–12 months from application to award), reporting overhead
- **Can be combined with Scenario A or B**

## Sensitivity Analysis

### Bull Case (Year 3: $5M+ ARR)
- NIH DMS enforcement drives rapid adoption
- Land 2+ large pharma enterprise deals ($15K+/mo each)
- International expansion (EU GDPR creates same demand)
- NRR > 130% (customers expand from Research → Consortium → Enterprise)

### Base Case (Year 3: $2.5M–3.5M ARR)
- Steady academic adoption, 2–4 enterprise customers
- 15% free-to-paid conversion
- NRR ~110–120%
- Break-even by late Year 2

### Bear Case (Year 3: $1M–1.5M ARR)
- Slower enterprise adoption (long sales cycles)
- Free tier cannibalizes paid (self-hosted open source is "good enough")
- Competitor (Flywheel or new entrant) adds defacing
- NRR < 100% (churn exceeds expansion)
- **Mitigation:** Reduce burn, focus on Research tier, pursue SBIR grants
